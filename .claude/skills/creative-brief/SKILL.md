# Creative brief

The creative brief is one layer downstream of the campaign brief. The
campaign brief says *what* we're trying to do; the creative brief says
*the idea* that's going to do it.

---

## Template

```markdown
# Creative brief — {concept name}

- **Linked campaign brief:** CB-YYYYMMDD-{shortname}
- **Concept ID:** CR-YYYYMMDD-{shortname}
- **Author:** creative-director
- **Status:** draft | options-presented | approved | in-production
- **Last updated:** YYYY-MM-DD

## 1. The job (one paragraph)
What are we trying to make the audience think, feel, or do — and why
will the world be slightly different after they see this work?

## 2. The single thought
One sentence. If you need a comma, you need a rewrite.

## 3. The big idea
The unifying creative idea that can flex across every channel. State
it in plain language; don't describe execution yet.

## 4. Why this idea, for this audience, now
- **Audience truth:** (from persona)
- **Cultural / category context:** (why this lands now)
- **Why we, not anyone:** (only-we-can-say-this proof)

## 5. Tone & feel
- **Voice attributes pulled from `brand-voice`:**
- **Tone shift for this campaign:** (e.g. drier, warmer, weirder)
- **References (mood):** 3 examples of work that feels like this. Note
  what we're borrowing and what we're not.

## 6. Mandatories
- **Must include:** logo lockup, legal lines, claim substantiation, etc.
- **Must NOT include:** banned phrases, competitor mentions, etc.

## 7. Deliverables (executions)
| Channel | Format | Word/length budget | Owner agent | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | |

## 8. Concept options
Present 2–3 distinct concepts under this same brief. For each:
- **Name:**
- **One-sentence pitch:**
- **Headline + subhead example:**
- **Visual direction (one paragraph):**
- **Why this concept wins:**
- **Risk:**

## 9. Review & approval
- [ ] Strategy lead
- [ ] Brand
- [ ] Legal (if claims/regulated)
- [ ] Exec sponsor
```

---

## Working rules

1. **Idea before execution.** Do not write headlines for a concept
   until the single thought + big idea are locked.
2. **Two or three options, not one and not seven.** Real options must
   be genuinely different — not three colors of the same idea.
3. **No mood-board-only briefs.** Every concept must have at least one
   written line of copy and one written line of visual direction so
   reviewers can judge the idea, not the polish.
4. **Cite the campaign brief.** Every claim about audience, KPI,
   message must trace back to the campaign brief — if it doesn't,
   either update the campaign brief or drop the claim.

## Handoff

Once approved, the creative-director hands the locked concept to:
- `copywriter` for all written executions (use `landing-page-copy`,
  `email-sequence`, `social-post-pack` skills as appropriate),
- `designer` for visual direction and asset prompts,
- channel agents (`paid-ads`, `lifecycle-email`, `social-community`,
  `pr-comms`) for channel-native adaptations.

All downstream deliverables must reference the Concept ID.
