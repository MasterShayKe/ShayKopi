# Audience personas

This skill is the single source of truth for who we're marketing to.
Every campaign brief and every piece of copy must declare a primary
persona by ID (e.g. `P1-smb-ops-lead`).

> **First-time setup:** populate at least one persona below. Without
> real personas the team will produce competent-but-generic work.

---

## Persona schema

Each persona is one block with this shape:

```
### P{n}-{shortname}  (e.g. P1-smb-ops-lead)

- **Role / segment:**
- **Demographics:** (only what matters — age, geo, company size, title)
- **Context:** what's their day like, what tools do they use
- **Goals (top 3):**
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** "When ___, I want to ___, so I can ___"
- **Pains / frustrations:**
- **Triggers to act:** what event makes them start looking
- **Objections:** the top 3 reasons they'd say no
- **Buying role:** decider / influencer / blocker / end-user
- **Channels:** where they actually pay attention
- **Vocabulary they use (and avoid):**
- **Proof they trust:** case studies, peers, analyst, demo, free trial
- **Last validated:** YYYY-MM-DD, source (interviews, survey, sales notes)
```

## Usage rules

1. Every brief must list **one primary persona** and at most one
   secondary. If a campaign needs three, it's two campaigns.
2. Copy and ads cite the persona ID in their header metadata.
3. If a deliverable mentions an attribute that isn't in the persona
   doc, flag it — either the persona is incomplete or the copy is
   speculating.
4. Re-validate personas at least quarterly or after any major
   product/positioning change.
